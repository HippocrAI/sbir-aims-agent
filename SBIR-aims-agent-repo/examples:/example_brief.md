## DEVICE

A non-invasive optical sensor that integrates with standard surgical retractors to provide continuous tissue oxygenation monitoring during gastrointestinal anastomosis. The sensor uses near-infrared spectroscopy (NIRS) to measure regional tissue oxygen saturation (StO2) at the anastomotic site and provides real-time visual feedback to the operating surgeon via a sterile-draped display. The form factor is a thin flexible probe that clips onto existing surgical retractor blades and communicates wirelessly with a bedside display unit.

## CLINICAL PROBLEM

Anastomotic leak following colorectal resection occurs in 3–15% of cases depending on technique, patient factors, and surgical approach, with associated 30-day mortality of 6–22% when leaks occur. Current intraoperative perfusion assessment relies primarily on (1) visual inspection — subjective and operator-dependent — and (2) indocyanine green (ICG) fluorescence angiography, which provides only snapshot perfusion data, requires specialized imaging equipment ($150K+), is not available at most centers, and does not enable continuous monitoring through the critical creation and testing phase of the anastomosis. There is currently no commercially available device for continuous quantitative monitoring of anastomotic perfusion during gastrointestinal surgery.

## COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

Indocyanine green fluorescence systems (Stryker SPY-PHI, Karl Storz Rubina, Olympus VISERA) provide single-time-point perfusion assessment but require dedicated imaging towers and are not designed for continuous monitoring during anastomotic creation. Tissue oximetry systems exist for cerebral monitoring (Masimo O3, Medtronic INVOS) and for free-flap monitoring postoperatively (ViOptix T.Ox), but no surgical-retractor-integrated solution is commercially available for intraoperative GI anastomotic monitoring. Wearable continuous tissue oximetry research devices have been described in the literature (academic prototypes only) but none have received FDA clearance for surgical use.
